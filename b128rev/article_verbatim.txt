How to Get Startup Credits Without an Accelerator
Field Notes on Agent Commerce
Most founders hear the same advice about free cloud money: join an accelerator, get the perks. It is true that a few big programs unlock their largest packages through accelerator partnerships, but a large share of vendors hand out credits to any startup that applies directly. You do not need a badge to get a slice. The catch is that very few founders go looking, because the programs are scattered across vendor sites that all describe them differently.
The numbers back this up. The open registry 
Sourcey
 currently tracks 607 companies in its startup credits catalog, and 427 of them run a program you can apply to yourself. Hosting and infrastructure vendors lead with 75 tracked companies, followed by AI and machine learning tools at 62. Productivity, security, CRM, and developer tooling vendors fill out most of the rest. Each entry carries the source URL and the date the fact was read, so you can check anything against the vendor's own page.
Some of the best known names accept direct applications. AWS Activate runs its own two tracks, Founders and Portfolio, with different eligibility and credit limits. Devin for Startups gives approved teams 65,000 dollars in credits to build with the AI engineer. Baseten runs an AI Startup Program for teams building on inference infrastructure, and AssemblyAI has a dedicated startup track of its own. None of these require an accelerator affiliation to apply.
Eligibility rules are usually simple. Most programs ask for a company website, a short description of what you are building, and a founding date within a few years. Some cap the credit amount by funding stage. A few want you to be incorporated in a specific country. Almost none require accelerator backing, so read the program page instead of assuming. The application itself is often a five minute form: company name, site, one line on what you do, and how you plan to use the vendor.
Discovery is the hard part, not eligibility. Programs change their caps, pause intake, or move their application pages without warning. This is where an open data registry earns its keep. 
Sourcey
 publishes its whole catalog as a downloadable JSON file under a CC BY 4.0 license, which means you can query it, sort it by category, and cross check the provenance of every fact. It also grades vendors on agent readiness, in other words whether a service can be signed up for, paid for, and operated by software working on its own. If you are building automation that buys tools, that grade matters as much as the discount.
A few practical tips before you apply. Keep a plain description of your company and a founder email ready, since most forms ask for the same fields. Track what you applied to, because credits often arrive as codes that expire quietly. Prefer programs that publish their terms and eligibility rules openly, since those are the ones most likely to still exist when you redeem. And apply for tools you would actually use, because a credit you never spend is just a line item.
Free credits will not save a weak product, but they can stretch a runway by months, and the direct programs are not reserved for the connected few. With more than 400 self applied programs live right now, skipping them to chase one accelerator interview is a choice, not a requirement.
